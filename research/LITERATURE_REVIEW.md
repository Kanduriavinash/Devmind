# DevMind literature review

**Review cutoff:** 16 November 2026  
**Prepared:** 17 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Position the implemented DevMind system and identify experiments it can honestly support. This is a scoped review, not a claim that every paper in software repair has been surveyed.

## Evidence and access notes

Primary conference/journal papers, proceedings pages, official benchmark material, and author preprints were used. Full text was inspected for the benchmark and methods papers where a PDF or HTML copy was accessible. The full-text page for the 2026 *RepoAI* journal article returned HTTP 403 in the original review; its publisher metadata and abstract were accessible, so that row is explicitly limited to the abstract. IQLoc has a 2026 journal version and an arXiv v2 manuscript; the reported numbers below are labeled as arXiv v2 figures and must not be copied into the final paper until checked against the journal version. Preprints are marked as such. On 17 November 2026, publisher metadata was rechecked for RepoAI and the Andrade et al. bug-report classification study; the latter is cited below by its 2026 journal version, while full-text access remains limited.

Numbers in this review describe the cited studies only. They are not DevMind results and are not directly comparable when datasets, models, budgets, or grading protocols differ.

## What this review establishes

1. Repository-level bug localization, report query reformulation, lexical/semantic ranking, hybrid methods, structured repair workflows, and execution-gated patch selection are established research directions.
2. Existing systems already link retrieval to later repair and measure effects under controlled conditions. Loc2Repair is an especially direct prior study for a broad “does localization help repair?” claim.
3. The literature distinguishes file-level localization from function/line localization and both from end-to-end patch resolution. DevMind currently returns ranked chunks and does not have independently labeled localization results.
4. Keyword extraction, camel/snake tokenization, category heuristics, TF-IDF, and optional embeddings are implementation components, not research contributions by themselves.
5. A defensible student-project result can be a reproducible, small-scale evaluation of explicit deterministic query variants and resource use on a verified issue subset. It must be framed as an empirical case study/replication exercise, not a new general method.
